Suno co-founder and CEO Mikey Shulman published a blog post laying out four principles behind the company's approach to AI-generated music, paired with several concrete new actions: an upcoming downloads policy aimed at curbing mass distribution to streaming platforms, audio watermarking and fingerprinting technology, and clarified community guidelines.

## Details

- **Four principles**: (1) "Great music is made by people" — technology can't replace the human emotion and imperfection that make music meaningful, and Suno runs weekly writing camps with artists to inform its tools; (2) "Technology opens new possibilities for creatives" — supported through educational programs and the Spark program, which gives unsigned artists grants, mentorship, and marketing support; (3) "AI should enable originality, not imitation" — Suno says it deliberately excluded artist names from training metadata and has never allowed prompts for specific artists or copyrighted songs, screening uploads via Audible Magic, ACRCloud, and Musixmatch; (4) "More people creating music should strengthen the ecosystem" — while most personal use is legitimate, the company acknowledges the risk of mass-generation abuse
- **New downloads policy**: A forthcoming policy designed to "limit the ability to mass distribute songs on streaming platforms" while preserving normal professional, creative, and personal use of Suno
- **Transparency and identification tools**: Suno is rolling out tools aligned with emerging industry provenance standards so AI-generated songs can be identified as they move across platforms
- **Audio watermarking and fingerprinting**: New durable, tamper-resistant watermarking and fingerprinting technology, designed not to affect the listening experience, aimed at combating fraud and unauthorized redistribution
- **Updated community guidelines**: Clarified rules explicitly prohibiting recreating existing songs, uploading unlicensed material, unauthorized use of an artist's voice or likeness, and scams/spam/fake engagement, with enforcement ranging from warnings to permanent bans
- **Framing**: Shulman frames the post as a response to industry-wide questions that "are bigger than any one company," calling for collaboration among artists, songwriters, labels, publishers, and platforms — the post follows Suno's ongoing copyright litigation with major labels and a July 2026 data-breach disclosure

## What happened next

- The downloads policy, transparency tooling, and watermarking system are described as rolling out rather than immediately live for all users; exact timelines were not specified in the post
- Suno says further details on these initiatives will follow as they roll out
