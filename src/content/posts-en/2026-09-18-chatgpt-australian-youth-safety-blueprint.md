OpenAI introduced the Australian Youth Safety Blueprint, a six-pillar framework meant to guide safer AI experiences for teens, positioned as a contribution to Australia's AI policy landscape.

## Details

- **The six pillars**: AI literacy, age-appropriate safeguards, privacy-protective age assurance, connections to real-world crisis support, accessible parental controls, and company accountability for identifying and addressing risks
- **Existing measures cited**: ChatGPT for Teens, launched in August with safeguards designed around the developmental needs of 13-to-17-year-olds, plus existing parental controls, under-18 safety policies, and age-assurance mechanisms
- **Stated commitment**: OpenAI says it will keep "learning alongside teens, parents, educators, researchers, and policymakers," and argues companies must build protections into products from the outset rather than placing the safety burden primarily on young people or families
- **Framing**: OpenAI describes Australia as having "an opportunity to help shape strong, evidence-based standards," without detailing why Australia specifically was chosen as the blueprint's focus

## What happened next

The blueprint extends OpenAI's pattern of engaging proactively with youth-AI-safety policy — following its earlier public support for California's SB 1119 — into a specific national context, positioning the company as a source of policy input ahead of, rather than in reaction to, Australian regulatory action.
