Suno announced a global partnership with artist-development company Believe and its self-releasing platform TuneCore on September 8, 2026, opening a distribution pathway to Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, and YouTube for tracks created through Suno's industry partner model.

## Details

- **Reversed prior refusal**: Believe and TuneCore had previously declined to distribute Suno-generated music over concerns about the platform's model standards; Suno says the partnership followed "constructive engagement" between the companies
- **Distribution pathway**: tracks made under Suno's industry partner model become eligible for distribution through Believe and TuneCore to major streaming platforms
- **New artist experiences**: the deal creates product experiences that Believe and TuneCore artists can opt into, giving them ways to collaborate with fans and get paid for AI-assisted work
- **Builds on Spark**: the partnership complements Suno's existing Spark program, which offers funding, mentorship, and marketing support to independent and emerging artists
- **Content safeguards**: Suno points to its existing audio watermarking, off-platform fingerprinting to identify Suno-made songs, and download limits as the trust mechanisms underpinning the deal
- **No financial terms disclosed**: the announcement does not specify royalty splits, licensing terms, or rights-ownership structures

## What happened next

No specific launch date was given for the new distribution features. The deal marks a notable shift in the music-distribution industry's posture toward AI-generated content — a company that once refused to carry Suno's output is now building a formal pathway for it — and lands one day before Suno's own v6 model announcement, suggesting the company timed its industry-relations push to coincide with a broader model upgrade.
