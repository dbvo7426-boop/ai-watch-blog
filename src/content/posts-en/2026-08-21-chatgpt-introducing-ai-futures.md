OpenAI has launched AI Futures, a new blog published by its recently formed Strategic Futures team, focused on how free societies can preserve individual rights and agency while accommodating the rise of transformative AI.

## Details

- **What it is**: AI Futures is described as "the blog of OpenAI's new Strategic Futures team," a small internal group rather than a public program or product.
- **Central question**: the team's work centers on "how should free society be restructured to preserve individual rights and agency while accommodating the emergence of transformative AI?"
- **Why this risk category**: OpenAI frames "concentration of power" as the AI risk it considers "the largest, most serious, and most conceptually challenging to address" — distinct from more commonly discussed risks like misuse or misalignment.
- **The underlying concern**: throughout history, political power has depended on human cooperation — soldiers, police, taxpayers, bureaucrats. OpenAI argues autonomous systems and machine intelligence could let states project force "without relying on cooperative soldiers" and generate revenue from data centers rather than human labor, structurally weakening the checks that cooperation has historically placed on power.
- **Six guiding principles** the team says will shape its work:
  1. Individual autonomy should be preserved in how people use AI, even when that means trading off some security or economic growth.
  2. Personal responsibility should accompany expanded technological access.
  3. Collective action should be reserved for serious, narrowly scoped risks.
  4. Law should empower individuals and small organizations relative to centralized power.
  5. Human political, social, and economic institutions should retain primacy in directing world affairs.
  6. "Bounded legibility" mechanisms are needed so that high-stakes AI actions can be traced to a responsible human, while still protecting ordinary privacy and anonymity.
- **Format and approach**: content will span blog posts, papers, videos, and podcasts, with what OpenAI calls "an emphasis on iteration, debate, and receptivity to feedback." The team says it will draw on public policy, economics, law, history, and machine learning, and explicitly states that "no single company, industry, or society can answer [these questions] unilaterally."

## What happened next

AI Futures joins a run of governance-focused posts OpenAI has published in recent days, including pieces on strengthening democratic oversight in national security (Aug 19) and pacing model development against cyber capabilities (Aug 19) — suggesting OpenAI is consolidating its policy commentary into a dedicated, recurring channel rather than one-off posts. The launch post itself contains no specific essays yet beyond the framing above; further AI Futures content is expected to follow on openai.com/index/introducing-ai-futures and related URLs.
