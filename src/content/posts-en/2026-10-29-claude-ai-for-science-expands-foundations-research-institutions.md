Anthropic expanded its AI for Science program, which gives free Claude credits to researchers on high-impact projects, to a group of 20 foundations, institutions and research groups worldwide.

## Details

- **Program:** Launched in May 2025; Anthropic says it has supported hundreds of projects across biology, medicine, physics, chemistry, climate science and mathematics.
- **Funders and societies (credits to distribute):** Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, Simons Foundation, Schmidt Sciences, Open Source for Science Fund (Renaissance Philanthropy), US National Science Foundation, and CAS (American Chemical Society).
- **Institutions (credits used internally):** Space Telescope Science Institute, Wellcome Sanger Institute, Francis Crick Institute, Imperial College London Department of Life Sciences, EMBL and Gladstone Institutes.
- **Initiatives (direct credits):** FlyWire, LEAP, AstroPath, gnomAD, Tau Ceti (Lean math library), and the Centre for Population Genomics in Australia.
- **Earlier partners:** HHMI Janelia developed the Model Hardware Standard for AI agents operating lab instruments (research preview); the Allen Institute is building agent teams for its open datasets.
- **Other:** A discounted Team plan for scientists launched in the months before. Credit amounts are undisclosed.

## What happened next

The change is about distribution: funders and institutes now hand out credits themselves. Anthropic says many collaborations are early-stage, plans to report on impact and hopes to add partners worldwide.
