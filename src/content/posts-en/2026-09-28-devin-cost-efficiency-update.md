Cognition announced on September 28, 2026 that Devin is now substantially cheaper to run across every mode, crediting newer underlying models and a series of harness-level efficiency improvements rather than a simple price cut.

## Details

- **Pricing changes by mode**: Fusion and Normal modes are 30-40% cheaper, Ultra mode is 15-20% cheaper, and Devin Review is up to 70% cheaper than before
- **Model lineup driving the change**: Devin now draws on newer models including SWE-2, Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, and GPT-6 Luna, with Cognition noting that "different models have different strengths, and new releases can change what's possible at a given cost"
- **Tool call batching**: newer models can bundle multiple operations, such as formatting, linting, and testing, into a single request, which Cognition says cut token consumption by roughly 49% in illustrative scenarios
- **Prompt caching**: reusing context across agent sessions cut tokens processed from scratch by about 71% in sample workflows, aided by improved cache pricing from providers including Anthropic and OpenAI
- **Benchmark performance**: Devin Fusion leads the FrontierCode 1.1 Extended leaderboard with a score of 68.8, at an average cost of about $0.60 per task

## What happened next

The price cut arrives the same day Cognition made Claude Sonnet 5.5 available inside Devin, and just three days after the company disclosed it had crossed $1 billion in annualized revenue run rate. Rather than resting on that growth, Cognition is using its expanding menu of frontier models and infrastructure optimizations to push Devin's cost-per-task down while keeping benchmark performance at the top of its own FrontierCode leaderboard, a combination aimed at making autonomous coding agents cheaper to run at enterprise scale even as usage grows.
