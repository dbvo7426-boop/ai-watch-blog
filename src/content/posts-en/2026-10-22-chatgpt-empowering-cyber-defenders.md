OpenAI published part II of its Democratic governance of frontier AI series, a blueprint for empowering cyber defenders with proposals for governments and industry. A companion post, Broad access through consistent standards, describes concrete changes to how partners that distribute OpenAI models must apply safeguards.

## Details

- **Defender's window:** OpenAI argues there is a limited opportunity to put advanced AI in defenders' hands before advanced offensive cyber capabilities become widely available.
- **Blueprint pillar 1, safety and security for advanced AI:** clearer responsibilities for securing frontier models and agents, detecting dangerous behavior, and keeping reliable shutdown controls for operations under each organization's control.
- **Pillar 2, modernizing cybersecurity:** advanced tools, training and technical support for defenders, fixing longstanding weaknesses, and investing in securing essential services and replacing outdated systems.
- **Pillar 3, resilient public institutions:** stronger government evaluations and independent technical assessments, consistent incident reporting requirements, and better coordinated response and recovery.
- **Partner standards:** Policies require partners (hosts and resellers) to maintain safeguards consistent with OpenAI's, investigate violations, act on concerns and coordinate enforcement. OpenAI is working with each partner to check implementations meet or exceed its standards.
- **Usage Policies update, effective November 20, 2026:** services offering direct access to models, such as model routers or AI gateways, must get OpenAI review and approval first.
- **SafetyIDs:** Customers can supply identifiers so OpenAI can distinguish end users in a misuse investigation without learning their identities.
- **Enforcement stance:** Decisions based on documented conduct and risk, applied consistently and proportionately, and revisited if conduct, commitments or safeguards change.

## What happened next

The blueprint is a set of proposals, not commitments binding anyone, and OpenAI urges policymakers to act now and tighten requirements over time. It cites its own misalignment and security incidents as motivation. The partner-standards changes are more concrete: the Usage Policy requirement for routers and gateways takes effect November 20, 2026. The list of affected partners and enforcement details were not disclosed.
