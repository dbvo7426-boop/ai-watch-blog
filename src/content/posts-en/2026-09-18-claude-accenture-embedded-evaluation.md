Anthropic partnered with Accenture to place independent evaluators inside its own organization with employee-level access, fulfilling a commitment CEO Dario Amodei outlined in an earlier essay calling for external oversight of frontier AI development.

## Details

- **What "embedded" means**: unlike traditional outside auditors, these evaluators work inside Anthropic with the access level of an employee, letting them observe model development during training, understand deployment decisions, and engage directly with staff
- **Investment**: Anthropic and Accenture plan to invest at least $1 billion over five years to build out this evaluation capacity
- **Who runs it**: Faculty, Accenture's specialist AI division, leads the partnership
- **Scope**: model evaluation, red-teaming, alignment assessments, and safeguard testing
- **Accountability framing**: Anthropic states that "independent embedded evaluators do not reduce our accountability, but help to make it more verifiable. The safety of our models remains our responsibility"
- **Open questions**: standards for evaluator access levels and reporting mechanisms are still undetermined, and both companies say they'd prefer a long-term model funded by pooled industry or government money rather than direct company-to-evaluator financing
- **Not exclusive**: Anthropic intends to work with multiple evaluators, including its existing partnership with METR, with further partnerships expected to be announced later

## What happened next

The scale of the commitment — over $1 billion across five years for a single evaluator relationship — signals Anthropic treating embedded, employee-level external oversight as a structural part of how it develops frontier models going forward, rather than a one-off audit, while leaving room for other evaluators to take on similar embedded roles.
