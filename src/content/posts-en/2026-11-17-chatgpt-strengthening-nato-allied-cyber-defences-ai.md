OpenAI described its work with NATO and allied cyber agencies to help trusted defenders find vulnerabilities and test fixes.

- NATO: through the NATO Communications and Information Agency (NCIA), NATO's core institutions have access to advanced cybersecurity models; NATO has used up to 300 billion raw input and output tokens per month.
- Exercise: OpenAI supported the 2026 Crossed Swords exercise run by the NATO CCDCOE, adding a training stream where participants built, deployed and evaluated AI agents for cyber operations using OpenAI Daybreak.
- Other defenders with access: the EU's ENISA and national cyber agencies in France (ANSSI), Germany and Poland. Ukraine's Ministry of Digital Transformation uses Daybreak for civilian infrastructure defense.
- ENISA used the models to identify four vulnerabilities in software used across EU institutions; fixes were published for all four.
- A November ENISA and CERT-EU report reviewed 27 software projects with frontier models (including OpenAI models via Daybreak and the Codex security plug-in). Of 88 potential issues assessed by maintainers, 95% were confirmed genuine.
- CERT Polska used OpenAI models to help find six vulnerabilities in MikroTik RouterOS; MikroTik released fixes, which CERT Polska confirmed stopped the attacks it had observed.
