Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a new entity to which it is committing a growing share of company equity instead of directing that equity toward escalating executive compensation.

## Details

- **Initial commitment**: subject to stockholder approval, 5% of Anthropic's shares are committed today; the board can add 2% for every 50% increase in the company's value above an undisclosed baseline
- **Total scale**: combined with earlier pledges, roughly 20% of Anthropic's shares will be dedicated to the public good, plus any additional percentage triggered by future valuation milestones
- **Structure**: the Fund is a wholly-owned LLC rather than a 501(c)(3) or other traditional nonprofit format, which Anthropic says gives it flexibility across different forms of giving; none of the equity held by the LLC will be used for lobbying or political giving in the US or abroad; Anthropic says it will share more on governance and valuation milestones in the coming months
- **Intended uses**: large donations to traditional nonprofits and direct distributions to specific populations, with labor-market impact named as a priority area — Anthropic argues AI could create large economic value alongside instability for many workers, and had already committed $200 million separately to this issue
- **What stays inside the company**: work with both public-benefit and for-profit value — AI safety and interpretability research, applied biomedical research, and discounted Claude access for nonprofits and scientific institutions — remains part of Anthropic's core operations rather than moving to the Fund
- **Context**: builds on the co-founders' original pledge to donate 80% of their equity, and an employee equity-donation match program in which roughly half of employees participate; Anthropic says it will also publish a public benefit report on progress against its mission

## What happened next

The announcement formalizes how Anthropic, as a public benefit corporation, intends to scale its mission-driven giving independently of its core commercial operations, redirecting a substantial and growing equity stake away from traditional compensation structures — a concrete step beyond its public benefit corporation charter that will be tested once Anthropic publishes the Fund's actual governance structure and valuation triggers in the coming months.
