Google launched Fairwind, a limited-access cyber defense program that pairs its Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber model with CodeMender, its automated vulnerability-remediation tool, aimed at finding, verifying, and fixing vulnerabilities at agentic scale.

## Details

- **Core capability**: Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber handles code analysis to find and verify vulnerabilities, while CodeMender generates deployment-ready, verified patches — Google says this cuts remediation time from weeks to minutes
- **Who it targets**: government agencies and national cyber authorities, critical infrastructure operators in healthcare, telecommunications, energy, and finance, core technology platforms, and Google Cloud customers and cybersecurity partners
- **Operational controls**: deployment happens within secure cloud environments, with access restricted to internal security teams and multi-factor authentication required
- **Access**: currently limited to roughly 650 global partners in early access, launched September 2, 2026
- **Broader availability**: Google Cloud customers outside the Fairwind program can still use CodeMender with publicly available models through the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, alongside its AI Threat Defense solutions

## What happened next

Fairwind positions Google as running a defensive-cyber-AI program parallel to OpenAI's $1 billion Daybreak for Frontline Defenders initiative announced two days earlier, with both companies now offering subsidized or specialized AI tooling to the same class of resource-constrained defenders — critical infrastructure operators and governments — as frontier models' offensive cybersecurity capabilities keep climbing.
