OpenAI's Economic Research Team published Work at the Frontier: AI's effect on freelance work, produced with the freelance marketplace Upwork. It compares Upwork job postings with the activities people perform in ChatGPT.

## Key findings

- **Different task mix:** writing made up a much larger share of ChatGPT use, while video and animation made up larger shares of Upwork job requests.
- **Shifting demand:** of 63 matched work subcategories, 43 followed the pattern where activities common in ChatGPT use lost share on Upwork and less common ones gained share. The other 20 showed the reverse, so ChatGPT use alone does not explain changes in freelance demand.
- **More AI-related requests:** by late 2025, clients sought AI work in many more kinds of projects, including chatbot development, video production, marketing automation and application integration.
- **Combined tasks:** AI-related postings listed about as many tasks as other postings but more often combined tasks that rarely appeared together before.
- **Greater diversity:** from early 2023 to late 2025, the task mix in AI-related postings became 45% more diverse, equal to rising from about 15 equally common tasks to about 21 (14.6 to 21.2).
- **Budgets and hours:** posted hourly rates were similar for AI-related and other work, but AI-related projects had larger fixed-price budgets and more hours, which may partly reflect broader scope.

## Method and caveats

The study compared ChatGPT use from January 2025 to June 2026 with Upwork shares for the first half of 2026. The authors note that the ChatGPT comparison reflects current usage, not the full range of tasks AI could support, a gap they call the capability overhang. They say the results do not show a simple substitution effect.
