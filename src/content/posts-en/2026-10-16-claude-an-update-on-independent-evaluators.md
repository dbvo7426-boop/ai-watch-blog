Anthropic began giving the independent nonprofit METR employee-like access to its models and internal systems to assess frontier AI risks, following its earlier commitment to stronger third-party assessment and its signing of the White House Accord on Super Intelligence.

## Details

- **METR access**: six METR employees have company hardware, office space, and internal tools roughly comparable to Anthropic's own risk-assessment teams, with Anthropic staff encouraged to share information except in defined categories like customer data, privileged legal information, and model architecture
- **Publication rights**: METR will publish its analysis of Anthropic's risk levels, incidents, and practices; Anthropic cannot veto publication but can require redaction of narrow categories, and METR may publicly state whether it considers the redactions justified without revealing the redacted content
- **Scope limits**: the METR team is small, works under enforceable agreements, and is walled off from other METR staff — the arrangement does not extend access to other organizations
- **Other evaluator partnerships**: SecureBio is reviewing the chemical and biological weapons chapter of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report, following an earlier pilot review; Accenture and Faculty are working toward a broader partnership covering red-teaming, alignment assessments, safeguard testing, and capability building; early discussions are underway with other organizations including Hugging Face
- **Three-tier framework**: Tier 1 covers narrowly scoped investigations with Anthropic controlling access and approving publication; Tier 2 gives more information and publication rights without Anthropic's approval, subject to redaction of specific categories; Tier 3, currently only METR's arrangement, grants employee-like access and publication rights similar to Tier 2 — Anthropic says it hopes several evaluators eventually reach Tier 3
- **Rationale**: Anthropic says it cannot publish all safety-relevant information since some could aid malicious actors or is commercially sensitive, so independent evaluators with deep access can assess and report on risks on the public's behalf; it compares the model to bank supervision, where regulators place examiners inside large banks

## What happened next

Anthropic explicitly frames this as voluntary and says "every frontier AI company should do the same," extending the institutional-oversight philosophy behind its earlier safety cases framework and Cyber Verification Program into direct, publication-empowered third-party access — a more concrete and higher-stakes step than most AI labs' external red-teaming arrangements to date.
