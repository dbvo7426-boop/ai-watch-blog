Sakana AI has announced Sakana Aquarium, a platform for building, running and operating AI agents that support an organization's decisions and their execution. It organizes scattered information into shared definitions through an ontology, and AI agents then handle the steps from checking information to making judgments and acting.

## Details

- **Ontology:** Organizes the knowledge, rules and data behind an organization's decisions together with their meanings and relationships. It connects tables, documents and other formats without moving them out of their existing storage, and reflects updates so agents can decide on current information.
- **Governance:** Controls data, ontologies and agents. Access permissions and pre-execution approvals can be set, and everything from the basis of a decision to the result of execution is recorded. Execution speed, accuracy and model usage can also be monitored.
- **Managed Agents:** The execution layer where agents run tasks based on ontology information. Referencing information, AI judgment, function execution and human approval are described as a workflow and deployed, with the full agent lifecycle managed.
- **Reuse:** Data connections and the ontology, once prepared, can be reused by other agents, so agents for different operations can be launched quickly and managed on the same foundation.
- **Background:** Sakana AI says the product consolidates work that forward deployed engineers repeated project by project in finance and other accuracy-critical fields, such as connecting data and organizing terminology and decision criteria.
- **Roadmap:** Mechanisms to feed corrections and decisions from operations back into the ontology and agents, and AI assistance for building agents, are in development. Use cases will be introduced later.

## What happened next

Aquarium moves Sakana AI from project-based implementations toward a reusable product layer for enterprise agents. The emphasis on approvals, audit records and traceable decisions targets regulated sectors where accountability matters. How widely it is adopted will depend on the use cases Sakana AI says it will publish.
