OpenAI announced "Daybreak for Frontline Defenders," a $1 billion commitment to subsidized access to its frontier cyber AI capabilities over the next six months, starting in the United States before expanding to partner countries.

## Details

- **Who it targets**: resource-constrained organizations defending essential services — water and wastewater system operators, electric grid operators, state and local governments, community and regional banks, nonprofits, and open-source maintainers
- **Subsidized access**: no-cost or reduced-cost API credits and Daybreak cyber models that let defenders review legacy code, analyze suspicious activity, identify vulnerabilities, and develop tested fixes
- **Training and support**: hands-on assistance through convenings with utility companies representing 40 states and Washington D.C., plus a pilot partnership with the Multi-State Information Sharing and Analysis Center (MS-ISAC) offering guided training for public-sector and water-system defenders
- **Daybreak Defense Network**: more than 35 enterprise products and partner-operated services now integrate OpenAI's cyber models directly into existing defender workflows and tools
- **Scale so far**: thousands of defenders across roughly 2,000 approved organizations already use Daybreak, and OpenAI has previously provided up to $1 million in no-cost API credits to individual states or utilities affected by attacks

## What happened next

The six-month consumption window starts immediately in the U.S., with OpenAI planning to expand the program to partner countries in the coming weeks and continuing its series of convenings with frontline defenders — positioning subsidized defensive cyber AI as a companion effort to GPT-6 Astra's newly disclosed offensive-capable cybersecurity strength.
