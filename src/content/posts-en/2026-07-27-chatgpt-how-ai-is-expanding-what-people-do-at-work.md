On July 27, 2026, OpenAI published a research report analyzing over 800,000 work-related ChatGPT messages from U.S. users, finding that 43.5% of occupation-specific tasks involved work outside a person's own job function. The findings quantitatively confirm what OpenAI calls "task crossover" — professionals routinely using AI to do work traditionally associated with other roles.

## Details

- **Scope**: Analyzed 800,000+ work-related ChatGPT messages from U.S. users, distinguishing generic tasks (writing, scheduling) from occupation-specific ones, then checking whether users were performing tasks outside their own occupation
- **Overall crossover rate**: 16.8% of all work-related messages, and 43.5% of occupation-specific messages, involved tasks outside the user's own field. Excluding generic tasks, nearly half of occupation-specific usage crosses job boundaries
- **Crossover by occupation**: Customer experience workers topped the list at 77%, followed by designers (75%), HR professionals (69%), legal professionals (56%), and marketers (53%)
- **Which tasks travel farthest**: Marketing- and engineering-related tasks showed up most broadly across other occupations. Financial calculations and technology troubleshooting ranked among the top "outside" tasks across all seven occupation groups studied
- **Company size effect**: Smaller companies (2-5 employees) showed an 18.9% outside-task usage rate, versus 16.3% at larger organizations (100+ employees) — suggesting AI serves as a generalist tool where specialist staff aren't available
