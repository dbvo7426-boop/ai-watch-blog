OpenAI launched the Grid Protectors Program, an effort to work with utilities, regulators and policymakers on arrangements under which its future compute campuses reduce electricity demand when the grid is under strain.

## Details

- **Approach:** Design campuses to reduce power use when needed, and agree with each utility on how much demand can be cut, how quickly and for how long. Arrangements will differ by location; OpenAI says it will share more commitments as they are finalized.
- **Supply side:** Depending on local needs and equipment, OpenAI will also work with grid operators to supply power from on-site generation or storage, and use batteries and power controls to smooth demand changes.
- **Georgia pilot:** For the planned Effingham County campus, OpenAI committed to pay the full infrastructure and electric-service costs. Its agreement with Georgia Power includes up to 1,000 megawatts of flexible demand response, which OpenAI equates to the average demand of roughly 700,000 households. Effingham is intended to be the pilot for the program.
- **Utility figures:** Georgia Power projects about $950 million in annual customer savings from 2029 across its large-load agreements and expected growth, with an estimated benefit of at least $15 a month for a typical residential customer using 1,000 kWh.
- **Cost principle:** OpenAI says large facilities should pay their own costs, and that long-term contracts and financial assurances can protect other customers if a project is delayed, reduced or canceled.
- **Undisclosed:** Campuses beyond Georgia, other utility partners and timelines were not named.

## What's next

The only concrete commitment so far is the Georgia agreement; the broader program is a stated ambition. OpenAI itself notes that flexibility cannot replace new energy supply and transmission investment.
