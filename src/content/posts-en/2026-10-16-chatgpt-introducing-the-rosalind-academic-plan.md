OpenAI introduced the Rosalind Academic Plan, a program giving eligible US academic labs free access to frontier OpenAI models and its dedicated life-sciences model, GPT-Rosalind.

## Details

- **GPT-Rosalind**: an existing OpenAI model built for deeper reasoning on complex biological questions, used alongside the Rosalind workbench, which orchestrates specialized tools for medicinal chemistry, genomics, and wet-lab assistance; access requires trusted-access approval with safeguards for sensitive research
- **What the plan offers**: up to 30 Business Standard seats at $0 per user per month for up to 12 months, access to all commercially available frontier OpenAI models (subject to plan usage limits), and access to GPT-Rosalind after trusted-access approval; research data shared through the plan is not used to train models by default; API access is not included
- **Eligibility**: currently limited to academic labs at US universities; PIs and lab leads at universities without an existing OpenAI Enterprise agreement can apply through the trusted-access process, while labs at universities with an Enterprise agreement must apply through their university's Enterprise access process
- **Vetting process**: OpenAI verifies the institution's legitimacy, the PI's identity and authority, the research activities and intended use, and the lab's institutional affiliation, and requires a designated institutional safety contact before activation
- **Management**: once approved, the PI or lab lead manages up to 30 named members' permissions and is responsible for the lab's approved use, with monitoring and enforcement continuing after activation

## What happened next

The plan extends OpenAI's push to seed academic adoption of its models — following patterns like its earlier mathematics research disclosure — by lowering the cost barrier for university labs specifically in the life sciences, while keeping GPT-Rosalind itself gated behind individual trusted-access approval rather than opening it broadly.
