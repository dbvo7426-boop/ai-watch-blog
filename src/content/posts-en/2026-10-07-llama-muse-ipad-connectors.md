Meta announced on October 7, 2026 that its Muse personal AI agent is now available as a dedicated iPad app, roughly a month after Muse debuted on iPhone and Android. Alongside the iPad release, Meta significantly widened the list of third-party services Muse can connect to and act on behalf of users.

## Details

- **iPad app**: the new version is built around the iPad's larger display and recent multitasking capabilities, rather than being a simple scaled-up phone app
- **Small-business connectors added**: Asana, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, QuickBooks, GitHub, Klaviyo, and Zoom, plus a connector for Meta ad accounts
- **New productivity connectors**: Notion and Granola join the lineup as well
- **Retail partners**: Meta had recently added Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Walmart, and Wayfair, letting Muse shop those retailers' sites and complete purchases on a user's behalf (subject to approval)
- **Install momentum**: since launching on iOS and Android on September 8, 2026, Muse has topped 6.6 million installs according to estimates from Sensor Tower
- **Core use case**: Muse continues to position itself around managing email, meetings, bills, reservations, and purchases through linked accounts

## What happened next

The iPad launch and connector expansion arrive as Meta pushes Muse beyond a single-device chat assistant toward a broader task-completion layer that reaches into small-business software and e-commerce. It follows Meta's recent move to open Muse to third-party hardware via the Muse Gadgets SDK, and comes just a day after a TIME investigation raised privacy concerns about how much personal and relationship data Muse retains on both users and the people they talk to. With millions of installs in its first month and a rapidly growing connector list, Muse's footprint — and the amount of third-party account access it requests — is expanding quickly.
