OpenAI CEO Sam Altman addressed the United Nations Security Council on AI safety, human control, and international cooperation, framing AI as a choice between Renaissance-like opportunity and Industrial Revolution-style disruption.

## Details

- **Core message**: "AI should be built for people, not simply to turn the crank of the machines faster"
- **Two risks he flagged**: loss of control, where systems advance faster than institutions can monitor them — stating "we should not train models that we cannot make an extremely strong case that we will be able to keep under human control" — and power concentration, where the technology consolidates influence in too few hands
- **Rejecting the race excuse**: Altman said competitive pressure should not justify safety compromises, noting OpenAI has "unilaterally slowed down in the past" and will do so again
- **Three stated principles**: people must remain central to AI decisions and human values can't be automated away; benefits must accrue broadly rather than narrowly; and the technology should empower individuals rather than replace their agency
- **International proposals**: complementary national and international frontier AI standards, unified capability/risk-assessment frameworks, incident-reporting protocols for global learning, secure government-to-government vulnerability-sharing channels, and standards that work for both open and closed model developers — emerging through democratic processes rather than corporate labs alone

## What happened next

The remarks extend OpenAI's recent cluster of governance statements — its global AI standards proposal and third-party assessment principles published earlier the same week — onto the UN stage, positioning international coordination on frontier AI oversight as a recurring theme across the company's public communications this month.
