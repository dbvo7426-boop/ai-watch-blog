Waymo has built Gemini directly into its custom Ojai autonomous vehicles, giving riders an in-cabin voice assistant that operates alongside — but fully independent of — the Waymo Driver self-driving system.

## Details

- **What Gemini does in the vehicle**: Riders can tap an icon on the in-car screen and use voice commands to control cabin features (Google's example: "turn the A/C to 65"), search for nearby businesses and amenities, learn about local landmarks and points of interest, and ask general-knowledge questions
- **Separate from the driving system**: Gemini runs independently of the Waymo Driver that actually operates the vehicle. It stays completely inactive until a passenger deliberately engages it, so it has no role in navigation or driving decisions
- **Activation is opt-in and manual**: There's no passive listening — riders choose when to bring Gemini into a ride by pressing the on-screen icon
- **Ojai is Waymo's custom vehicle**: The integration ships specifically in Waymo's purpose-built Ojai vehicles rather than the retrofitted third-party cars used in parts of its fleet
- **Rollout scope unspecified**: Google's post doesn't name which cities or how many vehicles have the feature yet, nor a timeline for wider availability; it points to Waymo's own "Waypoint" blog for further detail

## What happened next

This is one of the more concrete signs of Gemini moving into physical, real-world products beyond phones and browsers — an in-vehicle assistant riding alongside a live self-driving system. The open questions are rollout scope and whether Gemini's role expands beyond cabin control and local search into trip-relevant tasks over time.
