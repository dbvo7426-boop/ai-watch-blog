Google launched Guided Vision in Gemini Live, a voice-forward accessibility feature that gives real-time verbal guidance based on a user's live camera feed.

## Details

- **How it works**: once activated, Gemini analyzes the live camera feed and provides descriptive audio feedback, proactively guiding users with verbal cues such as "pan slowly to the right" or "step back" to optimize framing; users can ask natural-language follow-up questions about their surroundings
- **Problem it solves**: addresses everyday visual tasks for people who are blind or have low vision, such as reading nutrition labels, locating objects, identifying colors, and navigating unfamiliar spaces; Google also notes benefits for older adults and people with low literacy
- **Vs. standard Gemini Live**: specializes in real-time visual interpretation with verbal reframing cues tuned specifically for accessibility use, rather than general-purpose conversation
- **Development**: built in partnership with Aira, a provider of visual interpreting services, with input from more than 1,000 blind and low-vision testers
- **Availability**: rolling out on compatible Android devices (Android 9+) wherever Gemini Live is supported, with multi-language support across regions including India, Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, and Japan

## What happened next

Guided Vision extends Gemini's live multimodal capabilities toward accessibility-specific use cases, following a similar pattern to OpenAI's and Anthropic's recent moves to apply frontier models to concrete, narrowly scoped human needs rather than general-purpose chat — and lands the same week Google is also pushing Gemini 4 Argon toward professional and security-focused workflows.
