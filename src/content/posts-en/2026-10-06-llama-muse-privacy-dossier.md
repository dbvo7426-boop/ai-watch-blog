TIME published an investigation on October 6, 2026, based on a review of Muse's internal instructions, finding that Meta's personal AI agent — now used by roughly 4 million people — continuously builds and updates behavioral and relationship profiles on its users, and even on people who don't use Muse at all.

## Details

- **Social mapping**: Muse records how a user and their contacts met, their shared interests, and "disputes and tensions and alliances" between them
- **Non-user tracking**: people who never signed up for Muse but are mentioned in a user's chats, messages, or connected emails can still be mapped and profiled by the system
- **Behavioral inference**: internal instructions show Muse inferring undisclosed user goals and patterns, with an example cited in the report reading "this user responds better to short nudges after 10 PM," generated through nightly analysis of each day's conversations
- **Data use**: Meta says these are "de-identified learnings" used to improve the product broadly, rather than raw profiles shared between individual users' systems
- **Meta's response**: a company spokesperson did not dispute TIME's findings, saying Muse "remembers what matters most to you ... so it can be a helpful personal assistant"; Meta notes users can export their data or ask Muse to "forget" information, though original messages can persist in chat history, and the report says internal guidance directs Muse not to volunteer that detail when users ask it to forget something

## What happened next

The report lands five weeks after Muse's September 2026 launch, during which it topped Apple's App Store charts and grew to millions of users, and just days after Meta opened Muse up to third-party hardware through the open-source Muse Gadgets SDK. It also follows earlier reporting that Meta engineers found and patched a vulnerability in Muse's virtual-machine sandbox shortly before launch. As Meta pushes Muse deeper into smart-home control and everyday tasks, the dossier-building findings add to scrutiny of how much the assistant knows — and retains — about both its users and the people around them.
