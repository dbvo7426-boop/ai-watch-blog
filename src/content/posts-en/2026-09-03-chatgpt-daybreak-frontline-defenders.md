OpenAI launched "Daybreak for Frontline Defenders," pledging $1 billion in subsidized access to its frontier cyber AI tools over the next six months, starting in the U.S. with plans to expand internationally, aimed at defenders who protect critical infrastructure but lack enterprise-scale security budgets.

## Details

- **Who it's for**: water and wastewater system operators, electric grid operators, state and local governments, community and regional banks, nonprofits, open-source maintainers, and other organizations with limited security budgets
- **Three components**: subsidized access to frontier AI cyber capabilities and Daybreak models, hands-on training and technical support, and strategic partnerships to integrate the tools into defenders' existing workflows
- **Two access tiers**: Daybreak Blue supports mainstream defensive work using standard models, while Daybreak Red provides specialized cyber models for more sensitive, technical tasks; thousands of defenders across roughly 2,000 approved organizations already use Daybreak
- **New public-sector partnership**: a pilot with the Multi-State Information Sharing and Analysis Center (MS-ISAC) pairs Daybreak access with guided training to help state, local, tribal, and territorial defenders validate findings and coordinate fixes
- **"Defense Factory" approach**: OpenAI describes a continuous, agent-first operation built on existing security and engineering tools to find and validate vulnerabilities and prepare tested fixes
- **Recent precedent**: following recent attacks on water systems, OpenAI already gave affected utilities up to $1 million each in no-cost API credits, Daybreak access, and technical assistance to review code and develop patches while keeping operations running

## What happened next

The commitment arrives the same week OpenAI disclosed GPT-6 Astra's Critical cybersecurity capability rating, framing Daybreak as the defensive counterweight to a frontier model OpenAI itself says can autonomously find and exploit zero-days — an attempt to get equivalent capability into the hands of the under-resourced organizations most likely to be targeted before attackers get there first.
