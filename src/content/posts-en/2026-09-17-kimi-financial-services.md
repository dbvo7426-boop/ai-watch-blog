Moonshot AI has launched Kimi for financial services, bringing professional market and company data straight into its Kimi assistant. The move is Moonshot's clearest push yet from frontier model releases toward commercial, vertical applications. No official Moonshot blog post was found for this launch, so this article is based on CNBC's reporting.

## Details

- **Licensed data partners**: Kimi users can access data from S&P Global Market Intelligence, Crunchbase, China's Wind financial terminal, the business database Tianyancha, and local financial news providers
- **Public data sources**: Kimi can also query the U.S. SEC's EDGAR filings system, the IMF, the World Bank, and the Federal Reserve's FRED economic database directly
- **No separate interface**: The financial data is built into the regular Kimi experience rather than a standalone product. In CNBC's test of the mobile app, how much data was available depended on the subscription tier
- **Pricing**: Kimi subscriptions range from 49 yuan (about $7.31) to 699 yuan (about $104.23) per month
- **Customers cited**: Moonshot named investment bank CICC and venture firm Hong Shan (formerly Sequoia China) as users. A Deutsche Bank Beijing branch manager appeared in Moonshot's promotional video, but the bank declined to say whether it is a client
- **Underlying model**: The service runs on Kimi K3, Moonshot's flagship model released in July

## What happened next

With finance as its first vertical, Moonshot is following the playbook of U.S. labs that have packaged their models with licensed data for analysts and bankers. The launch comes as Moonshot reportedly prepares a confidential Hong Kong IPO filing, which the company declined to comment on. It also comes shortly before OpenAI's September 30 distillation accusations, which could complicate Kimi's sales to regulated institutions outside China.
