Mistral AI announced a three-part infrastructure push on August 11, 2026: general availability of regional inference endpoints for Europe and the US, a new SLA-backed Priority Tier for production workloads, and a coalition of European enterprises committing to fund up to 1 gigawatt of European compute capacity by 2030. The company is also opening its serving platform to third-party open models for the first time.

## Details

- **Regional endpoints (GA)**: customers can now pin inference to the EU or the US via separate base URLs — `api.eu.mistral.ai` and `api.us.mistral.ai` — so requests stay in-region for data residency, regulatory, and latency reasons; regional endpoints are priced at 1.1x the global rate, while the default global endpoint carries no upcharge
- **Priority Tier (public preview)**: a committed service level for mission-critical workloads, offering custom rate limits and an uptime SLA; no separate pricing has been published while it remains in preview
- **Third-party model hosting**: Mistral is opening its platform to outside open-weight models for the first time, starting with Z.ai's GLM-5.2, which runs on the same infrastructure with the same regional controls and service commitments as Mistral's own models
- **European Compute Units (ECUs)**: a new mechanism for aggregating multi-year enterprise compute commitments into new European data center capacity; founding members include Amadeus, ASML, Capgemini, Caisse des Dépôts, and CMA CGM
- **Capacity targets**: Mistral says the coalition's commitments will underwrite 200 megawatts of European infrastructure by the end of 2027, scaling to a full gigawatt by the end of 2030
- **Positioning**: Mistral frames the package as turning "European AI sovereignty" into a concrete, SLA-backed product rather than a talking point

## What happened next

- Mistral has not published Priority Tier pricing or a full list of upcoming third-party models beyond GLM-5.2
- The EU/US regional split and the ECU compute coalition are the more consequential parts of the announcement to watch, since they signal Mistral positioning itself as sovereign infrastructure for European enterprises rather than only a model vendor
