OpenAI is expanding the ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program, which began as a pilot in July, with a goal of reaching 200,000 researchers worldwide (double the goal set at the pilot's launch). It also published a study of how scientists used ChatGPT during the pilot.

## Program changes

- Applications for the next round open Monday, November 23. OpenAI will award 50,000 new grants on a first-come, first-served basis to eligible researchers, replacing the lottery-based selection used in the pilot.
- Each grant gives the recipient and up to two collaborators free access to ChatGPT Plus, covering up to 150,000 researchers. The pilot used ChatGPT Pro; OpenAI says most participants would have stayed within Plus limits.
- Participants get access to OpenAI's frontier models and training on using ChatGPT in research. Data shared through the program is not used for training by default. Waitlisted researchers who qualify will be emailed before applications open.
- Since the July pilot, 20,000 researchers in 33 countries have joined.
- Related access routes named by OpenAI: ChatGPT Edu for universities, and GPT-Rosalind for biology (the Rosalind Academic Plan offers up to 30 seats at no cost for up to 12 months to eligible U.S. university labs, with access subject to a trusted-access approval). OpenAI also contributed 5 million dollars to a new Simons Foundation initiative on AI results in mathematics.

## Study: Science at the Frontier

- OpenAI's Economic Research team analyzed 580,186 scientific messages from 1,819 scientists in the pilot and surveyed 251 participants. Automated classifiers sorted messages by activity; the team did not read the underlying messages.
- Surveyed participants estimated that the same work at the same quality would have taken 1.9 times as long without AI. Of those who answered the discovery-timeline question, 98% expected AI to accelerate discovery in their field.
- 68% of scientists used ChatGPT to develop research ideas. In those messages, scientists led with a specific question 35% of the time, developed an idea together with ChatGPT 36% of the time, and asked ChatGPT to suggest a question 23% of the time.
- OpenAI notes that whether these expectations translate into faster discovery needs more research.
