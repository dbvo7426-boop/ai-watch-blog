Genspark has launched Media Studio, an all-in-one AI studio for making video. The company says a user can start from an idea and a few prompts, and an agent handles script, cast, sets, storyboard, voice, score and editing, producing a piece ready to publish.

## Details

- **Availability:** Available now inside Genspark at genspark.ai/media-studio. Pricing and plan limits are not disclosed in the announcement.
- **Director Agent:** An agent plans, writes, storyboards, checks and cuts the piece. Before rendering it agrees concept, script, storyboard and budget with the user, then runs generation, quality checks, editing and compositing.
- **Director models:** Claude Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 plan the piece, write the script and write code. GPT-6 Astra handles multimodal verification in the 3D director stage.
- **Generation models:** The agent orchestrates dozens of models across image, video, audio and 3D from more than a dozen providers, including Seedance 2.5, GPT Image, Nano Banana, ElevenLabs and Seed Audio. New models are said to be added as they ship.
- **Single canvas:** Script, characters, sets, storyboards, 3D blockout animation, TTS voiceover, music and sound effects live on one infinite canvas. Media Canvas lets users manage all assets after the first cut.
- **Editor tools:** A Photoshop-class layer editor, multi-track video timeline, AE-style Motion Studio, a 3D white-model director stage and an interactive film and game engine. Users can edit manually, and agents can call the same tools. Users can also circle part of a frame and describe a change.
- **Range:** Examples run from a 15-second ad to a 10-minute short drama, plus posters and interactive films.
- **Genspark Magic:** A "make the same" feature turns a piece the user likes into the starting point of a new one, carrying over look, pacing and mood.

## What happened next

Media Studio fits Genspark's pattern of wrapping many third-party models behind an agent rather than shipping a single in-house video model. The notable design choice is the pre-render agreement on concept, script, storyboard and budget, which targets the cost and wasted-generation problem common in prompt-to-video tools. Open questions are pricing, credit consumption per render, and how output quality compares with dedicated video tools, none of which the announcement discloses.
