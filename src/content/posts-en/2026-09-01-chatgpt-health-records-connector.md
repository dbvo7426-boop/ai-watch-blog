OpenAI launched a connector that lets healthcare organizations bring authorized Epic EHR data and public healthcare datasets directly into ChatGPT, aimed at clinical, research, and administrative teams.

## Details

- **EHR access**: authorized clinicians can pull patient context from Epic environments — appointment notes, lab results, medications, and specialist documentation — directly into ChatGPT, letting them ask questions like "What has changed since this patient's last visit?" and get a summarized answer
- **Public data plugin**: a separate Healthcare Public Data plugin connects to nine official sources including ClinicalTrials.gov, CMS Coverage, RxNorm, DailyMed, and PubMed; individual U.S. clinicians without an organizational deployment get limited access to this public-data plugin only
- **Two deployment modes**: organizations can either access EHR context from within ChatGPT directly, or embed ChatGPT into their existing EHR workflow
- **Privacy and compliance**: deployments include role-based access, single sign-on, and audit logs, require a Business Associate Agreement, and support HIPAA-compliant workflows under the ChatGPT for Healthcare governance framework
- **Launch partners**: AdventHealth, Cedars-Sinai, HCA Healthcare, and UCSF

## What happened next

Organizations interested in deploying the connector need to go through OpenAI's sales team, positioning this as an enterprise healthcare offering rather than a consumer feature — a continuation of OpenAI's push to embed ChatGPT directly into regulated, high-stakes professional workflows following its earlier expansions into education and finance.
