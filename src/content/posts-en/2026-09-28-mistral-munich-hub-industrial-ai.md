Mistral announced on September 28, 2026 that it is opening a new hub in Munich dedicated to Physics AI and Industrial AI, pairing specialized research teams with applied engineers who will work directly with German enterprise partners.

## Details

- **Focus areas**: the Munich hub houses research teams working on Physics AI and Industrial AI, alongside applied engineers embedded with enterprise partners
- **Built on the Emmi AI acquisition**: following Mistral's acquisition of Emmi AI in May 2026, more than 30 physicists and engineers joined the company, bringing expertise in computational fluid dynamics, structural mechanics, and multi-physics simulation relevant to German manufacturing
- **Named partnerships**: Mistral is working with BMW on crash simulations and engineering AI, and with Siemens Energy on industrial AI applications for heavy industry
- **Research tie-up with TU Munich**: a partnership with Technical University Munich will use TUM's wind tunnel facilities to build digital twins for automotive aerodynamics, aiming to fuse real-time experimental sensor data with offline computational fluid dynamics simulations for accurate real-time aerodynamic predictions
- **Sovereignty pitch**: Mistral reiterated its commitment to building one gigawatt of European compute capacity by 2030 and pitched its open-weight architecture as letting customers run models on their own infrastructure under European legal compliance
- **Quotes**: co-founder and CEO Arthur Mensch said "Mistral stands for powerful AI made in Europe," adding that "technological sovereignty is political sovereignty," while Bavarian state minister Dr. Florian Herrmann said the Munich choice "sends a strong signal for our position as a high-tech location"

## What happened next

Mistral said it is actively recruiting in the Munich region to scale up its Physics AI and Industrial AI teams. The move frames Germany — Europe's largest industrial economy, with deep manufacturing know-how across automotive, energy, aerospace, and advanced manufacturing — as a long-term technology partner rather than just another software market, extending the industrial-AI push that began with the Emmi AI acquisition earlier in 2026.
