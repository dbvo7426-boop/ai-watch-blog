Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises, scientists and consumers on November 10, 2026. It follows the October 28 announcement, when access began with cybersecurity defenders through the Fairwind Program. Google calls it its strongest model, with frontier performance in cybersecurity and long-horizon agentic workflows. It sits above Gemini 4 Argon, which remains the better fit for most software engineering and knowledge work; Carbon uses more tokens to reason deeply and run extended verification loops.

## Pricing

- **Introductory:** $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, the same as Argon.
- **From November 30, 2026:** $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, for the public-access Carbon model.

## Availability

- **Developers:** Gemini API paid tier in Google AI Studio, and Android Studio. The model ID is gemini-4-carbon.
- **Enterprises:** Gemini for business via API or the model dropdown, Google Antigravity and CodeMender.
- **Google AI Ultra subscribers:** rolling out in Google Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search.
- **Cybersecurity defenders:** Fairwind Program, on a rolling basis.
- **Scientists:** Google Antigravity's highest usage tier free for 6 months, on a rolling basis and subject to eligibility and selection.

## Results reported by Google

- Ranks #1 on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, at more than 2X lower cost per task than the next best external model.
- Ranks #2 on the Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark, at more than 6X lower average cost per task than the top model.
- Leads on SEC-bench Pro, ExploitGym and CWE-bench v1 for vulnerability finding, exploitation and patching.
- In simulated attacks, Carbon blocked 99% of trajectories from an adaptive red-teaming agent; the per-attempt breach rate fell to 0.8% from 3.1% on Argon. Google says prompt injection robustness remains an area for continued improvement.
- On a 375-task agentic honesty benchmark, Carbon passed 92% of tasks versus 70% for Argon.

Google also announced /swarm, a preview in Google Antigravity that turns one prompt into a coordinated team of agents, with Carbon handling deep-reasoning roles such as architecture, coordination and auditing.
