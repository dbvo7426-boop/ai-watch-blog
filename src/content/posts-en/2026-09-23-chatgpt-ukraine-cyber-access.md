OpenAI announced it is extending access to its Daybreak cyber defense program to the Government of Ukraine, supporting civilian infrastructure defense during the UN General Assembly.

## Details

- **What it provides**: help identifying vulnerabilities, investigating suspicious activity, and validating and testing fixes for civilian systems under attack
- **Target sectors**: hospital systems, the energy sector, and telecommunications — critical infrastructure that has been directly targeted
- **Who it supports**: Ukraine's national cyber incident response team, CERT-UA, which handled nearly 6,000 cyber incidents in 2025, alongside public and private entities protecting essential services
- **Quotes**: Sasha Baker, OpenAI's Head of National Security Policy, said "Ukraine is already on the front line, and its defenders need support now"; George Osborne, Head of OpenAI for Countries, said "Protecting civilian infrastructure means defending it against both physical and digital attacks"
- **Precedent**: OpenAI has previously extended similar cyber model access to France, Germany, Poland, and other European countries — Poland's CERT Polska used the tools to discover six router software vulnerabilities, all since patched by vendors

## What happened next

The Ukraine extension continues OpenAI's pattern of offering Daybreak access to governments facing active cyber threats to critical infrastructure, following its earlier $1 billion Daybreak for Frontline Defenders commitment and its subsidized-access agreement with the U.S. General Services Administration.
