OpenAI published a policy position calling for the United States to lead an international effort to develop shared technical standards for frontier AI, including for recursive self-improvement (RSI).

## Details

- **Proposed mechanism**: leverage AI safety institutes already established in more than 10 countries through CAISI (the U.S.'s Center for AI Standards and Innovation) and national counterparts, building on the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation, and Science created in 2024
- **Proposed standards areas**: capability measurement and evaluation baselines, risk assessment protocols, safeguard sufficiency criteria, RSI-specific progress evaluation, human oversight requirements for automated research, and incident classification and reporting frameworks
- **Existing contributions cited**: OpenAI points to its own research acceleration report and its misalignment reporting framework, published earlier in September, as initial building blocks toward these standards
- **Explicitly not regulation**: OpenAI states the proposed standards "would not be licenses, mandatory prerelease review, or approval requirements" — implementation would be left to individual national governments, making this advocacy for an approach rather than a binding commitment OpenAI controls

## What happened next

The proposal extends OpenAI's recent run of safety-transparency initiatives — the misalignment reporting framework, its Astra safety disclosures, and now this standards push — into an explicit call for government-level coordination, positioning OpenAI as trying to shape how frontier AI oversight takes institutional form before individual countries set divergent rules on their own.
